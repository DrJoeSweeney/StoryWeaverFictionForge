Following from [[Chapter Three: The Ship (The Fish Out of Water)]]

The British gunship spotted, the fish out of water flees... race into the reef for escape.

The HMS _Devastator_ cut through the turquoise water like a razor, her brass cannons gleaming in the tropical sun as she turned broadside. From the crow's nest, Lieutenant Thorne watched through his telescope as the small fishing boat he'd been tracking for two days made a desperate dash for the labyrinthine reef that fringed the island. The boat's skipper—a local fisherman with more courage than sense—had been smuggling contraband, or so the Royal Navy insisted. Thorne suspected it was mostly dried fish and rum, but orders were orders, and His Majesty's Navy was not in the business of distinguishing petty smuggling from treason when the tide of war demanded otherwise.

"The sloop is gaining, sir," Midshipman Blight called out, his voice too loud for the calm morning. "She's making for the coral atolls."

Thorne lowered his telescope. The fisherman's boat was indeed heading straight for the reef, a treacherous maze of coral heads and shallow channels where a ship of _Devastator_'s draught would founder like a whale on sandbars. "Well, let him play his hand," Thorne said, though his knuckles were white where he gripped the railing. "The Royal Navy doesn't chase pirates into dens of rattle-snakes. Unless of course, the Admiralty demands it."

He could almost see the fisherman's thoughts from here—any man who'd lived these waters knew the reef better than he knew his own wife's face. The coral formations rose from the sea like stone teeth, their surfaces jagged and unforgiving. The channels between them ran shallow and narrow, navigable only by those who'd grown up reading the water's subtle signs. The fisherman would have the advantage now, but advantage in a chase was a fickle thing, like wind direction or a woman's affections.

"Ready the starboard guns," Thorne ordered, though he knew it was a futile gesture. Firing at a fleeing vessel in these waters was as likely to hit the reef as the target, and the Admiralty frowned on wasting powder on coral formations. "And keep her at five knots. We don't want to run aground before we've seen this farce through."

The chase began in earnest as _Devastator_ followed the fishing boat into the reef's outer perimeter. The water changed colour here, shifting from deep blue to the milky turquoise of shallow water. Coral heads appeared, some breaking the surface, others lurking just beneath, waiting to tear out the _Devastator's_ hull like a great white shark tearing into a seal. The fisherman maneuvered with impossible skill, his small boat slipping through channels that seemed narrower than a man's shoulders.

"Christ," Thorne muttered, spitting tobacco juice into the sand. "The man moves through those rocks like he's walking on his own damned parlour floor. If only the Admiralty valued such competence over the ability to draft a requisition form that could bore a man to tears."

Behind him, Blight cleared his throat nervously. "Sir, perhaps we should break off pursuit? The reef is treacherous, and the prize is hardly worth—"

"The Admiralty didn't ask for your opinion on prizes, Mr. Blight," Thorne cut in. "They asked for us to intercept a smuggler. Intercept we shall, whether we catch him or not. It's the principle of the thing."

The principle of the thing, Thorne thought bitterly, was that a Navy captain who returned to port without having chased down his quarry would face questions, and questions led to inquiries, and inquiries led to retirement on half-pay. The chase, therefore, was not about catching the fisherman. It was about not catching the fisherman, but being seen to try. War was full of such absurdities, a great game played with men's lives where the rules were written by men who'd never seen action outside of a parade ground.

The fishing boat veered suddenly, taking a channel so narrow that _Devastator_ had to slow to a crawl. Thorne could see the fisherman now—a small figure standing at the stern, looking back at them with what might have been a mocking smile. The man knew they couldn't follow. He was safe, and Thorne was left with the humiliating prospect of admitting defeat to a superior officer who would undoubtedly blame the reef, or the tide, or the color of the moon, but never the strategic idiocy of sending a seventy-four-gun ship after a fishing boat in a coral maze.

"Very well, Mr. Blight," Thorne said, his voice flat. "We'll anchor here. Give the man an hour head start, then we'll turn about. Let's not give the Admiralty the satisfaction of thinking we ran aground chasing a rum-runner."

As _Devastator_ began the laborious process of turning in the confined waters, Thorne watched the fishing boat disappear into the maze of coral. The chase was over, though the war continued. Somewhere in the distance, a gull cried out, and Thorne wondered if it sounded like laughter. In these waters, everything sounded like laughter. Or maybe that was just the madness of chasing fish into a reef with a ship of the line. One never knew. One simply continued.
