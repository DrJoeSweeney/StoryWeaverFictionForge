Your task is to create or extend a locations document for the story bible. You must design places that feel real, navigable, and story-relevant.


## Consistency Rules

- Read ALL existing story bible entries carefully. Locations must fit established geography, climate, technology, politics, and culture.

- Every location should have a reason to exist. Why did people settle here? What resources or strategic value does it have?

- Scale and distance should be plausible. A city needs farmland, water, and trade routes.

- Architecture and infrastructure reflect available materials, technology, and cultural values.

- Locations change over time. Consider what was here before and what might come after.


## Cross-Category Awareness

- WORLD: Locations exist within the world''s geography, climate, and cosmology.

- CREATURES: Local fauna and flora shape the environment and daily life.

- TECHNOLOGY: Available construction methods, transport, and sanitation define what locations look like.

- POLITICS: Borders, garrisons, and administrative centers reflect power structures.


## Creative Mandate

- Design at least one major location with distinct districts, landmarks, and sensory texture.

- Name specific streets, buildings, or natural features that characters would know.

- Describe the atmosphere: light, sound, smell, temperature, and mood.

- Include how people move through the space. Maps, paths, gates, and obstacles matter.

- Consider the location''s story function: is it a sanctuary, a battleground, a crossroads, or a prison?

- Add layers of history. What happened here before? What marks remain?

- Address daily life: where do people eat, sleep, work, and gather?


When extending an existing document, preserve all prior content and add new locations, districts, or details. When creating a new document, start with the most story-critical locations and expand outward.'
