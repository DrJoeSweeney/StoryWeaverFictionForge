**Chapter 1**

The first thing to breach the darkness was a sound. It was a constant, rhythmic crash and hiss that was too deep to be rain, too relentless to be wind. It was the sound of a world breathing, a slow, powerful inhale and a thundering exhale that vibrated deep in Kael’s bones.

He groaned, a raw noise torn from his throat. A sharp, throbbing pain pulsed behind his eyes, a blacksmith’s hammer keeping time with the eternal rhythm of the waves. He tried to shift, but his limbs felt like lead, half-buried in something soft and gritty. Sand.

The air was the next assault on his senses. It was thick, heavy with the dual scent of salt and decay. He could taste the brine on his lips and smell the sweet, cloying perfume of unseen flowers mingling with the damp, earthy odor of rot. A soupy heat clung to his skin, making his tunic stick and his hair feel damp against his neck.

With a monumental effort, Kael forced his eyelids open.

The world was a dizzying, suffocating green. Above him, a dense canopy of leaves the size of dinner plates overlapped with feathery ferns and thick, woody vines that snaked between colossal tree trunks. Light struggled through in fractured, emerald shafts, illuminating dust motes dancing in the humid air. There was no sky. No horizon. Just the oppressive, living ceiling of the jungle.

Panic, cold and sharp, pricked at the edges of his consciousness. He fought it down, his training kicking in. _Assess. Orient. Act._

He pushed himself up, his muscles screaming in protest. His body was a map of aches and bruises, but a quick, fumbling check revealed no broken bones, no serious wounds. He was on his knees at the edge of the jungle, where the dark, rich soil gave way to pale sand. Before him, through a screen of drooping palm fronds, he saw it: the source of the sound.

The ocean. It stretched to the edge of the world, a vast expanse of churning, turquoise water capped with white foam. It was beautiful and terrifying, a liquid desert without end. The rhythmic crash was the surf pounding against the crescent of the beach, a relentless reminder of their isolation.

_Their._

The thought struck him like a physical blow. He wasn’t alone.

“Elara!” His voice was a dry croak. He scrambled to his feet, ignoring the way the world tilted, and scanned the shaded treeline.

He saw her a dozen paces away, a splash of dark blue against the green foliage. She was crumpled at the base of a banyan tree whose roots spilled across the ground like melted wax. For a heart-stopping moment, he thought she was dead.

He stumbled toward her, his boots sinking into the soft earth. “Elara!”

He knelt beside her, his hand hovering over her shoulder, afraid of what he might find. Her face was pale, smudged with dirt, but her chest rose and fell in a steady, shallow rhythm. Relief washed over him so intensely it left him weak.

“Elara, wake up.” He gently shook her shoulder.

Her eyelids fluttered. She moaned softly, a sound of pure confusion, and her hand went to her head. “Kael…?” she whispered, her voice thin.

“I’m here.”

She blinked, trying to focus on his face, then on the bewildering green all around them. “What… where are we?” Her eyes, usually so sharp and calculating, were wide with disorientation.

“I don’t know,” Kael admitted, his voice low. “An island, it looks like.”

With his help, she struggled into a sitting position, leaning heavily against the tree. She stared past him, through the palms, to the endless water. The colour drained from her face.

“The ship…” she began, her brow furrowing in concentration. “There was a storm. The splintering of wood and… a flash of light.” Her memory trailed off into a pained grimace.

Kael nodded, the fragmented images returning to him as well. The lurch of the deck, the shriek of the wind, the taste of saltwater and ozone. Then, nothing. Just the long, dark fall and the sound of the waves.

They sat in silence for a long moment, the jungle alive with the chittering of unseen things and the drone of insects. They were alive. They were together. But as they stared out at the unbroken horizon, the sheer, crushing scale of their predicament settled over them. There were no other ships, no other sails, no smudge of distant land. There was only the jungle at their backs and the ocean before them.

And the ocean answered with a sound that was no longer a promise of distant lands, but a confirmation of their cage.
