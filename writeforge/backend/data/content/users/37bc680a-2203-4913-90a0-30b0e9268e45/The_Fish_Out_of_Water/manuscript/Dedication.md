# Dedication

For Ray, whose eagle eyes spotted every reversed letter and wandering word, protecting readers from my dyslexia.

You learned the patterns of my unique chaos. My mind always insists that \*b\* and \*d\* are just doors swinging both ways, and sometimes whole clauses disappear from a sentence like startled birds, leaving only the punctuation behind. You never saw these moments as flaws to erase, but as riddles in a language only we tried to understand. In the margins, your questions became a second conversation—gentle and sharp—a light shining through the dark rooms of a manuscript where I had left things in disarray.

You gave my voice armour and my words wings.

And to Nila, my wife, who looked upon my peculiar madness and decided it was merely weather. You never asked me to tidy the chaos from my mind; you simply learned which rooms to shut and which to air. When I paced the house at midnight, conducting arguments with the dead, or when I became convinced that the postman was a metaphor, you did not reach for a cure. You reached for the biscuits. You understood that my dyslexia is not a broken latch on an otherwise sound door, but the whole house tilting slightly—and you moved your furniture in anyway. Where Ray gave order to my words on the page, you gave sanctuary to the disorder that produces them. Because of you, my insanity has never been a solitary haunting. You have made it a household: something that sits down to breakfast, leaves its shoes in the hall, and is loved not despite its odd angles but because of them. You are the reason the madness feels like music rather than noise. Ray gave my voice armour and my words wings. You, Nila, gave my insanity a home. Thank you.
