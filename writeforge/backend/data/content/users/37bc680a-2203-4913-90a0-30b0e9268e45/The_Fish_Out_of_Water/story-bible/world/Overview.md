Based on the provided documentation, the following description outlines the setting, background, and character dynamics for a narrative set in the world of Fish Out of Water.The Incident at CERNThe story begins in the early 21st century at the CERN supercollider. Ten honours students—highly educated specialists in fields ranging from pharmacology and materials science to law and physics—volunteered as test subjects for a theoretical physics experiment. The experiment involved being laid in hospital beds within a modified section of the collider, surrounded by massive, ambitious machinery.When the machines discharged, they produced a vibration felt in the chest and a light that appeared to originate from within the subjects' own skulls. Instead of the intended result, the experiment scattered the ten subjects across four centuries of time.The World of 1700A group of these students emerged on an uninhabited island in the Caribbean, roughly three hundred years before their birth. After twelve days of struggle, they were rescued by the Fish Out of Water, a French privateer vessel.The setting is the Golden Age of Piracy (circa 1650–1720), a world defined by:The Caribbean Frontier: A region of brutal sun, tropical storms, and constant maritime conflict.Colonial Powers: The landscape is dominated by the Spanish Empire, which controls the major ports, merchants, and massive warships, and the English, whose naval officers and colonial authorities provide constant friction for privateers.French Privateering: The protagonists operate within a French-speaking maritime culture, where the Fish Out of Water acts as a "floating republic with cannons".Harsh Realities: The era is defined by a lack of sanitation, the institutional existence of slavery, the treatment of women as property, and the use of child labor.The "Fish Out of Water" DynamicThe core of the narrative is the tension between specialist modern knowledge and historical impracticality. The protagonists are:Intellectually Elite but Practically Useless: They possess deep understanding of neurochemistry, structural fatigue, or legal theory, but cannot tie a nautical knot, fire a flintlock, or navigate by the stars.The Accent Problem: Every displaced person speaks with a conspicuously strange 21st-century cadence and rhythm that native speakers notice immediately. This creates a sense of unease and makes them permanent outsiders.Culture Shock: The students must reconcile their modern sensibilities with a world that views disease as divine punishment and resolves disagreements with a cutlass.Key CharactersEleanor Blackwood: An honours student in Experimental Pharmacology. She is methodical and clinical, attempting to synthesize medicines from Caribbean plants because she cannot tolerate the "barber-surgeons" who treat wounds with vinegar and tar.Thomas Carver: An honours student in Mechanical Engineering and Materials Science. He is a pragmatic man living in a state of chronic frustration; he can see exactly why the ship’s rigging is inefficient but lacks the 21st-century infrastructure to fix it.The Scattered Students: Eight other students are lost across time (from the 15th to the 18th centuries). Some are in danger, while others—particularly those who studied History—have used their knowledge of the future to become dangerous "villains of history," manipulating power and events for personal gain.The Crew: A mixed group of privateers who view the scholars with a blend of amusement, suspicion, and provisional hospitality contingent on their usefulness.The Ship as a CharacterThe Fish Out of Water is a nimble French privateer. In this world, a ship is not merely a setting but an entity with its own temperament. It is a place of "coordinated screaming" and "constant proximity of drowning," where the crew and vessel must function as a single instrument to survive.
